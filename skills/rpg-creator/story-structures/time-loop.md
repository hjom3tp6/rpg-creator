# Structure · Time Loop — the same day, until you change
cast_pattern: solo

The protagonist relives one stretch of time — a day, a voyage, a festival night — that ends in the same
disaster and resets. Only they remember. Knowledge is the only thing that accumulates, and the loop
breaks only when they understand why it exists and change more than events.

## Phase shape (5 stages)
1. **First pass** — the day as it happens, ending in the disaster and the first reset.
2. **Learning the day** — the player maps the schedule, exploits foreknowledge, saves small things.
3. **The wall** — every direct fix fails; the disaster adapts. Despair, recklessness, or a new angle.
4. **The key** — the real cause: a person, a promise, a wish, the protagonist's own part in it.
5. **The last day** — one run where everything learned is spent at once.

## Pressure (replaces the ticking clock)
The **fixed end of the loop** is the deadline every run, plus a **cost of looping** that ratchets
across resets (memories fraying, the reset spreading, something following the protagonist between
loops). The world resets; the cost does not.

## Opposition
The loop itself, the fixed event at its end, or someone else who also remembers — an ally or a rival
who wants the loop to continue.

## Endings it supports
The loop breaks because the protagonist changed · a sacrifice breaks it · acceptance (choosing to
stay) · escape with one other person who now remembers · the loop is revealed as a gift, not a curse.

## Cast pattern — solo with visitors
The protagonist carries the story alone; everyone else is a visitor who resets. The depth check is
**rebuilt relationships**: at least two characters whom the player must re-win each loop in a new way,
and one who begins to notice something is wrong.

## What a good beat looks like
Use something learned in a past loop to surprise someone — then find one detail never seen before.
Summarize known stretches in a line; spend prose only on the new.

## Laying out main-arc.md
- `## Phases` — the five stages; which discoveries unlock stage 3 → 4.
- Add `## The Day` — a schedule table: time · place · what happens unless changed.
- Add `## Reset Rule` — what triggers it, what carries over (memory; maybe one object), what ratchets.
- `## Pressure` — the loop's end and the ratcheting cost. Track loop count and known facts in `flags`.
- `## Opposition` — the loop's cause and any other rememberer.
- `## Presence Matrix` — visitors by time of day; mark who carries memory.

## Golden rules it swaps
- *Stakes ratchet, never reset* → **The world resets; knowledge and cost ratchet.**
- *Failure is a story* → **Failure is research.** Every death or disaster teaches something specific.

## Pitfalls
- Repeating prose — fast-forward anything the player already knows.
- A day too small to explore for many loops: give it 8–12 places and secrets.
- An unclear reset rule; state it in fiction early and never cheat it.
