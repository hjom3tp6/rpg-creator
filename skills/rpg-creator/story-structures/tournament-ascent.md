# Structure · Tournament Ascent — climb the ranks, one rival at a time
cast_pattern: revolving

An underdog enters a contest with a bracket, a ladder or a season — duels, races, debates, cooking,
music, a trial of masters. Each round is a new opponent and a new lesson. The real question is what
winning will cost and whether the prize is still wanted when it is within reach.

## Phase shape (5 stages)
1. **Qualifying** — enter as the overlooked one; earn a place by an unexpected method.
2. **Early rounds** — learn the field; the rival emerges; small wins build reputation.
3. **The setback** — a loss, an injury, a scandal, a disqualification threat.
4. **Recovery** — a mentor, a new technique, a reason that is the player's own rather than borrowed.
5. **The finals** — the rival, the crowd, the prize, and the price.

## Pressure (replaces the ticking clock)
**The bracket schedule:** the next match is a fixed appointment that cannot be skipped. Add **standing**
(rank, reputation, sponsorship) that rises and falls with how the player wins, not just whether.

## Opposition
Rival peers, each with their own reason to win — not villains. Optionally the institution running the
contest is corrupt, or a patron who wants the player to throw a match.

## Endings it supports
Win, and learn what it cost · lose the final but earn what mattered · refuse the final on principle ·
win and find the prize hollow · the rival and the player change each other.

## Cast pattern — revolving door
A constant coach or teammate; a new opponent each round; one recurring rival who appears in every
stage. The depth check is **turnover with a spine**: every round introduces a distinct opponent with a
stake, and the rival relationship changes in at least three stages.

## What a good beat looks like
A match is a conversation: how each side competes reveals who they are. Mechanics resolve the contest,
but the beat turns on a choice (a risky move, a mercy, a trick). Between matches: rest, training, and
the rival off the field.

## Laying out main-arc.md
- `## Phases` — the five stages; place the setback clearly.
- Add `## Bracket` — round · opponent · their style · their stake · what the player learns.
- `## Pressure` — the schedule and the standing track (mirror standing into `flags`).
- `## Opposition` — the rival's arc across stages; any corruption subplot.
- `## Presence Matrix` — coach constant, opponents per round, rival recurring.

## Golden rules it swaps
- *The antagonist moves off-screen* → **Rivals train off-screen.** Show them improving.
- *Every character is a gap* → **rivalry and ambition** carry the drives; respect is the payoff.

## Pitfalls
- Matches as single dice rolls — give each match 2–3 decision points.
- Every opponent a jerk; most should be admirable.
- A predetermined outcome — the finals must be genuinely winnable and losable.
