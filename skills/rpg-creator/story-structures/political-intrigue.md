# Structure · Political Intrigue — every conversation is a duel
cast_pattern: rotating

A seat of power — a court, a council, a merchant league, a ship's officers — approaches a decision that
will reshape it. Factions want different futures and trade favors, secrets and loyalty to get them.
The player is small at first; information and debts are how they grow.

## Phase shape (4 stages)
1. **Arrival** — the player enters the arena and maps it: who wants what, who owes whom.
2. **Alignments** — favors traded, a patron chosen or refused, a first secret bought or sold.
3. **The crisis** — the decision point arrives early or is threatened (a death, a scandal, an
   ultimatum). Alliances are tested; a betrayal lands.
4. **The reckoning** — debts are called in. The player's web of favors decides who stands where.

## Pressure (replaces the ticking clock)
**A scheduled decision** (a vote, a succession, a treaty signing) plus a **ledger of favors and debts**:
every request granted creates an obligation that will come due at the worst time. Information decays
— a secret is worth most before others learn it.

## Opposition
No single villain. Three to five factions, each with a sympathetic want and a ruthless method; one
rival peer competing for the same patrons. The institution itself is the arena and a player in it.

## Endings it supports
Kingmaker (the player decides who rules) · seized power · principled exile · a compromise peace that
satisfies no one · branching by allegiance (each faction's victory is its own ending).

## Cast pattern — rotating ensemble
Factions are the sub-casts: the player moves between salons, councils and back rooms, rarely meeting
everyone at once. The ensemble-depth gate applies; the "full cast" moment is the decision itself.

## What a good beat looks like
A conversation where both sides want something and neither says it plainly. The player leaves with
something gained and something owed. Ends on an offer, a threat dressed as courtesy, or a leak.

## Laying out main-arc.md
- `## Phases` — the four stages with the betrayal placed in stage 3.
- `## Pressure` — the decision date and the favor/debt ledger (track debts as `flags`).
- `## Opposition` — replace a single antagonist with a `## Factions` table:
  faction · want · fear · leverage · representative.
- `## Presence Matrix` — rotate faction sub-casts; the decision beat is the only full-cast beat.

## Golden rules it swaps
- *Thriller, not hangout* → **Every conversation is a duel.** Talk is action; a dinner can be a battle.
- *The antagonist moves off-screen* → **Every faction moves off-screen**, each on its own agenda.

## Pitfalls
- Too many factions (more than five blurs; fewer than three is a duel, not intrigue).
- Exposition about history instead of people wanting things now.
- The player as observer — they must hold leverage someone needs by the end of stage 2.
