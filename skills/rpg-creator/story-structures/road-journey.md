# Structure · Road Journey — the way there changes who arrives
cast_pattern: revolving

Someone must get from here to there: deliver a thing, reach a person, escort a passenger, flee. The
story is a chain of stops, each a small self-contained world that asks the traveler a question.
What waits at the end is never quite what they set out for.

## Phase shape (departure + 3–6 waystations + crossing + arrival)
1. **Departure** — why leave, what is left behind, what is carried.
2. **Waystations** — episodic stops (a town, a ferry, a festival, a ruin, a stranger's house). Each has
   its own small conflict and reflects the traveler's inner question back at them.
3. **The crossing** — the hardest stretch; a point of no return. Something must be given up to pass.
4. **Arrival** — the destination, and the discovery that the goal or the traveler has changed.

## Pressure (replaces the ticking clock)
Distance against supplies, or **a pursuer behind**, or **a promise at the destination** that can be
missed. Choose one primary; the road's weather and terrain add texture, not doom.

## Opposition
The road itself (terrain, weather, borders), a pursuer with their own reasons, or none at all — the
traveler's doubt about why they are going.

## Endings it supports
Arrival, transformed · choosing not to arrive · the return home, changed · arriving too late, and
finding a different purpose · the passenger was the destination all along.

## Cast pattern — revolving door
One or two constant companions; vivid locals at each stop who mostly do not return. The depth check is
**turnover with echoes**: each stop introduces at least one new character, the companions change
across the journey, and at least one earlier local returns late as a callback.

## What a good beat looks like
A stop presents a small problem the traveler could ignore; helping costs time or supplies; the locals'
choices mirror the traveler's own dilemma. Ends with the road calling — a reason to move on.

## Laying out main-arc.md
- `## Phases` — departure, the waystations in order, the crossing, arrival.
- Add `## Route` — stop · what it offers · what it costs · the question it asks.
- `## Pressure` — the chosen primary pressure and how it tightens toward the crossing.
- `## Opposition` — the road's dangers and the pursuer (if any).
- `## Presence Matrix` — companions constant or changing; locals per stop; mark returns.

## Golden rules it swaps
- *The mystery drips* → **Each stop asks one question;** the journey answers them together.
- *Rotate the cast* → **Revolving door:** locals are vivid and brief; companions deepen.

## Pitfalls
- Stops that don't connect — seed at least two threads that cross several stops.
- No reason to keep moving: if staying is free, pressure is missing.
- Companions who never change — the road must cost or teach them something too.
